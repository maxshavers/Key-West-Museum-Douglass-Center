A new anchor for Bahama Village

The island’s first school for African-American children has a new home

The center carries the name of the Frederick Douglass School, established in Bahama Village in 1871 and for generations the only Black school on the island for children in grades one through twelve. Today the building holds a museum, classrooms, music rooms and a hall for the neighborhood.

FAQs

What was the Frederick Douglass School?

Established in 1871 in Bahama Village, it was the island’s first school for African-American children and the only Black school in Key West for grades one through twelve. The center preserves and celebrates its history and legacy.

What will I see in the museum?

The Black Educators History Museum honors the teachers who taught Key West children through more than a century of segregated schools, with a collection of artifacts from the city’s first Black educators.

How can my child join the Bahama Village Music Program?

The program provides free music, singing and theater education to the neighborhood’s children, and now rehearses in the center’s music rooms. Enrollment details will be posted here.

Can I rent the event hall?

The center includes a 2,600-square-foot event hall, a commercial kitchen and a courtyard for community gatherings. Rental information will be shared by the City of Key West.

Where can I learn more about Bahama Village history?

Watch “Trekking Through Bahama Village,” featuring historian John Wilson Smith, or join the Key West Art & Historical Society’s Bahama Village walking tour.
